## Scope and Limitation of the Project

The Basilience is an IoT and Android-based fogponics cultivation system designed for Girasoles Farm Candelaria to monitor and support the cultivation of Genovese basil. The system will maintain stable root-zone conditions through fine-mist nutrient delivery, with droplets approximately <10 micrometers in size, within a semi-controlled fogponics setup. The proposed system will monitor critical nutrient-solution and canopy parameters and initiate automated regulation when selected values deviate from the recommended ranges for basil growth, supporting consistent plant development from seedling establishment through the vegetative stage. The fogponics process begins once established Genovese basil seedlings are transferred into the system. These seedlings will be raised separately using a nursery method with rockwool starter media through the third (3rd) week, distinct from traditional soil-based cultivation. The fogponics process then continues through vegetative growth and harvest readiness, from approximately week eight (8) to week nine (9) onward. The cultivation cycle management will reduce manual monitoring workload and generate records for evaluating plant growth and harvest output against the measured parameters.

The physical system architecture consists of a nutrient reservoir where atomization occurs and an enclosed root chamber where basil roots are suspended. The chamber measures approximately five (5) feet in length and two (2) feet in width, with an adjustable height of four (4) to six (6) feet to accommodate basil root growth over the cultivation cycle, and will space plants one (1) foot apart, consistent with the farm's traditional soil-based cultivation. Mist will travel through a PVC pipe, assisted by a 12V blower fan that supports mist circulation throughout the chamber. The canopy section will be semi-enclosed to support stable canopy-level conditions and reduce exposure to uncontrolled greenhouse airflow. The proposed system will use a comprehensive sensor suite to monitor and regulate key growing parameters. A pH probe will measure acidity across a range of pH 0 to pH 14 with an assumed accuracy of ±0.1 pH, while an EC sensor will measure nutrient concentration from 0 to 20 mS/cm with an assumed accuracy of ±2%. A DS18B20 waterproof sensor will measure nutrient solution temperature with an assumed accuracy of ±0.5°C, and a DHT22 sensor, positioned at the canopy level, will measure air temperature (±0.5°C) and relative humidity (±2% to ±5% RH). Reservoir water level monitoring will be performed using an HC-SR04 ultrasonic distance sensor mounted at the upper section of the reservoir. Sensor readings will be gathered through the ESP32 microcontroller every five (5) seconds for local monitoring and control checks.

The proposed system will evaluate acquired sensor data against predefined threshold ranges to determine when automated corrections are needed. These threshold ranges include pH level (5.5–6.5), electrical conductivity (1.2–2.0 mS/cm), nutrient solution temperature (18°C–25°C), air temperature (20°C–32°C), relative humidity (60%–75%), and lighting duration (14–16 hours/day). These values define the acceptable operational boundaries for Genovese basil cultivation. All automated regulation depends on an active cultivation cycle existing in the mobile application. If no cycle has been created, the system's automation will not run: threshold-based corrections, scheduled fogging, and grow-light operation will all remain inactive until a cycle is started. Manual Mode remains available regardless of cycle status, allowing an Admin to directly control actuators even when no cycle is active. Because more than one parameter may go out of range simultaneously, the proposed system will not run every correction at once. Safety actions will always come first. If the water level is too low, refill or a full safety stop will take priority over everything else, including stopping a pH or EC correction that is already running. The system will also prevent pH and EC corrections from running at the same time. Both will be checked every cycle, but only one will be allowed to run at a time. If both pH and EC are out of range together, pH will be corrected first, and EC will be corrected in a following cycle once pH's correction is done. This prevents the two dosing pumps from running together. Temperature correction will work independently from pH and EC, allowing cooling to operate simultaneously with a pH or EC correction.

To ensure system stability, strict safety mechanisms are enforced alongside the priority sequence. A low-reservoir-level safeguard will stop fogging, dosing, and cooling to prevent dry running. Following each correction attempt, a stabilization period will occur before re-measurement. A strict maximum of three (3) correction attempts per event will be enforced before the affected subsystem is locked and requires manual Admin reset. During a correction event, the system will keep sampling at the standard five-second rate, but the affected parameter will be temporarily held from further correction until a short delay passes, ensuring the system does not react to temporary changes caused by the last correction still mixing in.

For nutrient solution temperature, readings above 25°C will activate the Peltier module's cooling function, which will stay on until the reading drops to 23°C. This 3°C gap prevents rapid cycling near the threshold. The current firmware cools water only and does not yet support heating. For pH regulation, a reading above 6.5 will activate the pH Down pump for five (5) seconds, and a reading below 5.5 will activate the pH Up pump for five (5) seconds. Following each dose, the circulation pump will run for three (3) minutes to mix the solution throughout the reservoir. The system will check whether the reading has stabilized, allowing for normal sensor noise of approximately ±0.05 pH. If unstabilized, the circulation pump will continue cycling at thirty (30) second intervals until stability is achieved and a stable reading is obtained before the value is used for further evaluation. EC regulation compares the reading against the active range for growth. A high reading will activate dilution water for five (5) seconds, and a low reading will activate nutrient dosing for five (5) seconds, following the same circulation and stabilization protocol as pH correction. The peristaltic pumps will be calibrated during setup to an assumed flow rate of approximately sixty (60) mL per minute, delivering roughly five (5) mL every five (5) seconds—a consistent amount suitable for the reservoir's working volume of approximately 10.6 liters. Nutrient solutions (NutriHydro Grow for vegetative stages and NutriHydro Bloom as available) will be dosed according to the active growth phase.

A canopy-level DC brushless ventilation fan will run at all times to keep air moving and prevent stagnant conditions. It will speed up to full power when canopy air temperature goes above 32°C or humidity goes above 75%, returning to normal speed once the temperature drops to 26°C or humidity drops to 70%. It will slow to its lowest speed when air temperature drops below 20°C, returning to normal once temperature climbs back to 22°C. Fogging will be handled by an 8-head ultrasonic fogger with a rated output of approximately 4 to 5.6 kilograms per hour, paired with a 12V blower fan that assists mist circulation throughout the root chamber. The fogger will operate under four distinct states based on air temperature and system startup. During startup, the fogger will run for one (1) minute thirty (30) seconds, then rest for one (1) minute before switching to its regular schedule. In normal conditions between 20°C and 32°C, the fogger will run for five (5) minutes and rest for five (5) minutes. When air temperature rises above 32°C, the system enters hot state and the fogger will run more frequently at eight (8) minutes on and four (4) minutes off to help cool the plants. When air temperature drops below 20°C, the system enters cold state and the fogger will run less frequently at three (3) minutes on and five (5) minutes off. Fogging will always stop if the reservoir water level is too low, regardless of the active state. Bench testing showed no measurable decrease in reservoir water level over the observed testing period, suggesting that a significant portion of atomized mist condenses within the enclosed chamber and returns to the reservoir or root zone rather than escaping as lost vapor.

A dedicated Fogging Reports section will document watering activity in tabular form, recording each fogger activation event with its ON and OFF timestamps, run duration, and trigger type (Automatic or Manual). For automatic activations, the report will show which fogging strategy was active (startup, normal, hot, or cold), allowing users to review the schedule and cadence behind each activation. The same screen will include a Water Outlook feature displaying the reservoir's current level in percentage, centimeters, and liters, a rolling seven (7) day water consumption average, and an estimated time remaining before the next refill is required. This provides a clear, data-driven record of misting behavior and water use for checking watering frequency, system responsiveness, and environmental adaptability across the cultivation cycle. The system will stop all fogging, dosing, and cooling and raise an alert once water level reaches two (2) centimeters or lower. An automatic refill function will open a solenoid valve for thirty (30) seconds per attempt, pausing for a ten (10) second settling period between attempts, for a maximum of three (3) attempts. If refill times out after three attempts, the system will notify users and lock the refill subsystem, requiring manual Admin reset before automated refill can resume. Supplemental lighting will use two 100W full-spectrum grow lights on a fixed sixteen (16) hour schedule, turning on at 6:00 AM and off at 10:00 PM, sitting at the upper end of the fourteen (14) to sixteen (16) hour target range. This will run on a set schedule rather than reacting to sensor readings, though an Admin can manually turn the light on or off through the mobile application.

The Android mobile application will serve as the primary interface for real-time monitoring and operational awareness. Device pairing will use a token-based claiming process, with each physical device assigned a unique claim token at setup, tied to that specific device in the system's records. Once claimed, only the owner and assigned personnel can view live readings or send commands, as this is verified on the server. The Monitoring screen will display live sensor readings and the current status of each actuator, including pH and EC dosing pumps, the Peltier temperature module, canopy and reservoir fans, the fogger, and grow lights. An Admin can manually turn selected actuators on or off, temporarily overriding their automatic control. A personnel management feature will allow the device owner to assign other users (such as farm staff or consultants) specific roles and access levels, ensuring only authorized individuals can view sensitive data or make operational changes. A cultivation cycle management feature will let users set a start date for each cycle, choose how often harvests are expected (five days by default, adjustable per cycle), and manually record harvested basil weight in grams. The system will send a harvest reminder the day before and the day a harvest is due. A Harvest Log screen will plot recorded harvest weights as a cumulative yield chart, tracking weight over time throughout the cycle.

From the Reports section, users can open a Parameter Report, filtering cultivation conditions for the selected cycle by the entire cycle, today, the last seven days, the last thirty days, or a custom date range, and export results as a PDF for documentation and analysis. Alerts for parameter deviations and safety events will be delivered through in-app and push notifications when the device is online. When the device is offline, the same events will send SMS alerts, covering low reservoir levels, high water temperature, high air temperature, invalid sensor readings, device unreachability, and harvest reminders. An SMS alert threshold will be defined to prevent notification overload. To keep the system accessible to non-technical users, the mobile application will include a User Guide with step-by-step instructions for navigating the interface, and a dedicated hardware tutorial covering maintenance, sensor calibration, and manual nutrient solution replenishment, with embedded video tutorials and a catalog of every system sensor and its function. A Settings section will allow users to configure preferences, adjust notification settings, and manage account details. This localized, easy-to-understand guidance is intended to let farm staff with limited technical proficiency operate and maintain the Basilience platform smoothly and confidently.

The Basilience system is built specifically for Genovese basil, with predefined parameter ranges and control logic optimized for this crop. These parameter ranges are not adjustable through the mobile application; adapting the system to another crop would require firmware-level modification and recalibration. The system likewise will not include full greenhouse climate control, active air heating, evaporative cooling, dehumidifiers, humidifiers, or motorized ventilation, so environmental adjustment beyond fan-assisted airflow and the defined fogging behavior falls outside its scope. The system will have no pest detection, disease identification, imaging-based plant assessment, or automated crop-quality grading. While water refilling will be automated, the system will not automatically replenish nutrient stock solutions or pH Up/Down solutions, so mixing, probe calibration, and routine maintenance will remain entirely manual. The proposed system will not cover the full basil lifecycle starting from seed. Germination and early seedling development will continue to rely on a separate nursery method using rockwool starter media through the third week, outside the fogponics chamber. This is a deliberate scope boundary: fine-mist nutrient delivery depends on an already-developing root system with functional root hairs capable of absorption, which are not yet present at the seed or pre-germination stage. Extending automated coverage to include germination would require a separate, humidity-controlled propagation environment distinct from the fogponics chamber's vegetative-stage misting regime, which falls outside this study's scope. Finally, the system depends on reliable power and internet connectivity. Local ESP32-based monitoring and regulation continue through temporary internet outages, but remote monitoring, cloud synchronization, and manual overrides become unavailable. Offline SMS alerts remain subject to cellular network reliability. Extended power interruptions halt all monitoring and regulation and require manual intervention to recover. These outcomes reflect the principles of precision agriculture applied within the context of fogponics-based cultivation for Genovese basil at Girasoles Farm Candelaria.
